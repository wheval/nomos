## The short version

| | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Private payment (Flow A) | **0.10 USDC** / 4 STRK |
| Public payment (Flow B) | **0.20 USDC** / 8 STRK |
| Payout | **0.30 USDC** / 12 STRK |

No percentage. No monthly minimum. No setup fee.

A 10 USDC payment and a 100,000 USDC payment cost the same 0.10 USDC to accept.

## Why flat, and why that matters

Most payment processors take a percentage because their own costs scale with
risk and volume. Ours don't. Settling on Starknet costs the same whether the
payment is ten dollars or a hundred thousand — the privacy pool charges one
flat fee per operation, not a share of the amount.

Charging you a percentage on top of a flat cost base would be arbitrage, and
it would throw away the reason this stack is interesting in the first place.
Shielding through Railgun costs 0.25% of the amount. On a 100,000 payment
that is 2,500. Through Starknet's privacy pool it is around 0.12, regardless
of size.

So the pricing keeps the shape of the thing underneath it:

| Payment | Nomos | A 2.9% + 30c card processor | Railgun (shielding only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0.10 | 0.59 | 0.03 |
| 100 | 0.10 | 3.20 | 0.25 |
| 1,000 | 0.10 | 29.30 | 2.50 |
| 100,000 | **0.10** | 2,900 | 250 |

## Why a private payment is cheaper than a public one

The two checkout flows cost us different amounts, and the price reflects that
rather than averaging it out.

**Flow A** — the payer sends from a shielded balance. They pay the pool fee
themselves as part of their own transaction. Nothing is left for us to do
except pay out later.

**Flow B** — the payer sends an ordinary public transfer. That lands
unshielded, so we have to shield it before it can be paid out privately.
That is a second pool operation, at our expense, which is why Flow B costs
double.

The result is that the more private option is also the cheaper one. That is
deliberate.

## The payout fee

Paying out is a pool operation with a real cost — a pool fee plus gas —
charged per payout rather than per payment. Ten payments settled in one
payout cost the same to settle as one.

The 0.30 payout fee covers that, and it is why payouts have a minimum:

| | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Payment | 1 USDC / 40 STRK |
| Payout | 5 USDC / 200 STRK |

The payment minimum exists because a flat fee is an unreasonable share of a
very small payment. The payout minimum exists so payouts batch — withdrawing
after every single payment would cost you a settlement fee each time, for no
benefit.

Leave your balance to accumulate and withdraw in batches. That is the cheapest
way to use Nomos, and it is the same advice any processor would give you.

## What you are charged, and when

Fees come out of settlement. You never receive an invoice, and your customer
never sees a Nomos line item at checkout.

1. Your customer pays the full amount of the link.
2. We record the payment at its **gross** amount — what actually moved
   on-chain.
3. Your ledger is credited the **net**: gross minus the transaction fee.
4. When you withdraw, the payout fee is deducted from the amount you request.

Both the gross and the fee are stored against every transaction, so your
dashboard always shows what was paid alongside what you were charged. Changing
our fees later never rewrites what you were charged in the past.

## Fees are charged in the token that was paid

A USDC link is charged in USDC. A STRK link is charged in STRK. Your customer
never has to hold a second token, and neither do you.

STRK amounts are set to approximate the USDC ones at current prices and are
adjusted if STRK moves substantially. USDC amounts are fixed.

## Custody, stated plainly

Nomos holds funds between the payment and your payout, the same as any
payment processor. Stripe and Paystack hold your money for days; we hold it
until you withdraw.

What is different is what the public chain sees. With a traditional processor
your revenue is private from the public, but entirely visible to your bank and
your processor. With Nomos, payments are invisible **on-chain** — amounts,
senders, and your total volume are not public information.

Nomos itself can see them, because Nomos has to settle them. So the honest
statement is that your payments are private from the chain, not that they are
private from us. Non-custodial settlement, where funds never touch a Nomos
account, is on the roadmap and is not what is offered today.

If that trade-off doesn't suit you, the privacy pool is a public protocol —
you can integrate it directly and pay only the protocol's own fee. Nomos
exists to save you building checkout, links, verification, payouts, webhooks
and reconciliation on top of it.
