---
title: The two payment flows
description: What the customer picks at checkout, and how each one is verified before a merchant is credited.
---

Checkout offers the customer two ways to pay. Both settle into Nomos's
operating wallet; they differ in what the chain reveals and in how Nomos proves
the payment happened.

## Flow A — pay privately

A shielded STRK20 transfer, submitted by the customer's own wallet. The chain
reveals neither sender, recipient, nor amount.

That is also what makes it hard to verify: there is no Transfer event to read.
Nomos instead matches an incoming note in the operating wallet, and three
conditions all have to hold before a merchant is credited:

1. **The transaction exists on-chain and succeeded.** A fabricated hash is
   rejected before any note is looked at.
2. **A matching note was created in that transaction's block.** This ties the
   note to *this* payment rather than to any payment of the same size.
3. **The note has not already settled a deposit.** Claiming is atomic, so one
   note can never be credited twice, even under a race.

<Callout type="warn">
Matching on amount alone is not sufficient, and was the cause of a real
vulnerability during development: because the operating wallet is shared
custody, a note from someone else's payment satisfied an amount-only check.
</Callout>

## Flow B — pay with any wallet

An ordinary public ERC-20 transfer. No privacy-capable wallet needed, so it
works with any Starknet account.

The payment itself is public, but every merchant is paid through the same
operating wallet — so an observer sees a transfer, not which business it was
for.

Verification decodes the Transfer event on the claimed token and confirms it
paid the operating wallet at least the claimed amount. Two event shapes exist
on Starknet and both are accepted:

```
indexed   keys: [selector, from, to]   data: [low, high]
legacy    keys: [selector]             data: [from, to, low, high]
```

Reading only the indexed shape silently rejects Mainnet USDC, which uses the
legacy one.

## Settlement timing

Flow A credits the merchant immediately — the funds are already shielded.
Flow B lands as `pending_shield` until shielding is confirmed, because moving a
public payment into the pool requires a screening step that is currently
operated manually.
