---
title: The two payment flows
description: What the customer picks at checkout, and how each one is verified before a merchant is credited.
---

Checkout offers the customer two ways to pay. Both settle into Nomos's
operating wallet; they differ in what the chain reveals and in how Nomos proves
the payment happened.

## Flow A — pay privately

A shielded STRK20 transfer, submitted by the customer's own wallet. The chain
reveals neither sender, recipient, nor amount.

That is also what makes it hard to verify: there is no Transfer event to read.
Nomos instead matches an incoming note in the operating wallet, and three
conditions all have to hold before a merchant is credited:

1. **The transaction exists on-chain and succeeded.** A fabricated hash is
   rejected before any note is looked at.
2. **A matching note was created in that transaction's block.** This ties the
   note to *this* payment rather than to any payment of the same size.
3. **The note has not already settled a deposit.** Claiming is atomic, so one
   note can never be credited twice, even under a race.

<Callout type="warn">
Matching on amount alone is not sufficient, and was the cause of a real
vulnerability during development: because the operating wallet is shared
custody, a note from someone else's payment satisfied an amount-only check.
</Callout>

## Flow B — pay with any wallet

An ordinary public ERC-20 transfer. No privacy-capable wallet needed, so it
works with any Starknet account.

The payment itself is public, but every merchant is paid through the same
operating wallet — so an observer sees a transfer, not which business it was
for.

Verification decodes the Transfer event on the claimed token and confirms it
paid the operating wallet at least the claimed amount. Two event shapes exist
on Starknet and both are accepted:

```
indexed   keys: [selector, from, to]   data: [low, high]
legacy    keys: [selector]             data: [from, to, low, high]
```

Reading only the indexed shape silently rejects Mainnet USDC, which uses the
legacy one.

## Settlement timing

Flow A credits the merchant immediately — the funds are already shielded.
Flow B lands as `pending_shield` until shielding is confirmed, because moving a
public payment into the pool requires a screening step that is currently
operated manually.

## How a private payment is recognised

This is the part of Nomos with no equivalent in a public-chain gateway, and it
is worth understanding before you integrate.

A public transfer announces itself. It has a transaction hash, it emits an
event naming the sender, the recipient and the amount, and anyone can watch
for it. That is exactly what a payment processor normally builds on.

A shielded STRK20 transfer announces nothing. There is no event to watch, no
sender to read, and the amount is not public. What Nomos can see, through the
viewing key of the account being paid, is that *a note arrived* — carrying an
amount, and nothing else. Not who sent it, and not which payment link it was
meant for.

So the amount has to carry that information, because it is the only field
there is.

**Every payment attempt reserves a unique amount.** When a customer opens a
link for 25 USDC, Nomos records an intent and quotes them something like
25.000412 — the price plus a few millionths of a unit that no other open
attempt is using. When a note for exactly 25.000412 arrives, it names that one
attempt and no other. Attribution stops being a guess and becomes a lookup.

The overpay is bounded and tiny: at most 999 micro-units, under a tenth of a
cent in USDC. The same trick is standard for Bitcoin processors, where it
leaks the payment to anyone watching the chain. Here it does not — the amount
is shielded, so only Nomos, holding the viewing key, ever sees it.

Two consequences worth knowing:

- **The payer must send the quoted amount exactly.** The checkout handles this
  for you; if you drive the API yourself, use the amount the intent returns
  rather than the link's round figure.
- **Settlement does not depend on the payer's browser.** Because the note
  identifies itself, Nomos can find and credit it with no transaction hash and
  nobody watching. A customer who approves the payment and immediately closes
  the tab is still paid in. This matters more than it sounds: shielded wallets
  do not reliably return a transaction hash to the page that asked for it, so
  a gateway that depended on the browser would lose payments routinely.
